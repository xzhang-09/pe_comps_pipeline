8. Methodology Notes

Rank (Section 2) reflects overall comparability to the target: the standardized financial distance plus soft penalties (in the same distance units) for business-model, customer-type, revenue-scale, and end-market mismatch — not the EV/EBITDA multiple.

The Tier column is an independent fit-quality flag (from the Comparable Fit Review and the statistical outlier check), not a function of Rank. They usually agree, but a comp can be the closest financial fit (high Rank) and still be flagged for a second look (e.g. a different customer base) — the two are separate signals shown side by side on purpose.

The Comparable Fit Review (Section 3) is a directional qualitative review; it is not a substitute for transaction-team judgment, banker input, or confirmatory diligence.

Target Percentile (Section 4) shows where the target falls within the selected comp set. It is calculated directly from company-level data, so it may not match a simple interpolation from the rounded quartiles shown in the same table.

"Relative influence" (Section 6) values are standardized, weighted squared distances — comparable only to each other within that table (to show which financial attribute drove the ranking most), not an absolute or standalone valuation metric.

Geography filter. By default the pipeline restricts candidates to US-domiciled companies (per the FMP company profile's country field). Companies with no country data in FMP are kept rather than discarded. Non-US comps are excluded upstream and will not appear in this report.

End-market scarcity. For a niche target, comps that match on both size and exact end-market are often scarce, so the set leans on broader same-industry names. Where the closest end-market match falls just outside the Top {{ n_comps }}, it is surfaced in the near-miss list (Section 5) and the fit review's substitution suggestions (Section 3) rather than silently dropped.
